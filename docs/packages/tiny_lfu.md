# tiny_lfu notes

`tiny_lfu` ports Caffeine's `FrequencySketch` and `WindowTinyLfuPolicy` for Einziger, Friedman, and Manes's *TinyLFU: A Highly Efficient Cache Admission Policy* (arXiv:1512.00727; local copy `note/TinyLFU.pdf`). `FrequencySketch[K comparable]` is Caffeine's four-bit Count-Min layout: minimum 256 `uint64` slots, four probes in one 64-byte block, estimates capped at 15, and periodic halving after ten times the configured maximum. Growing reallocates and forgets; shrinking retains the table but retunes and clamps the aging counter. Hashing uses a per-sketch `maphash` seed, with `NewFrequencySketchWithHash` for stable or deterministic hashes.

`TinyLfu[K comparable,V any]` combines an LRU admission window, probation and protected SLRU main segments, and the sketch. Defaults are 1% window rounded up and 80% of main protected. Admission requires candidate frequency **strictly greater** than victim frequency; ties reject the candidate. Every `Put` and successful `Get` increments the sketch. A `Get` miss changes only `Misses`; the usual miss path is `Get`, load, then `Put`. `Peek` has no policy or stats effect. `Delete` preserves frequency history and is not an eviction. `Clear` retains configuration, history, and stats.

The weighted constructor uses total weight as capacity, retracks the sketch to live entry count at half-full just as Caffeine does, rejects negative weights, and discards entries heavier than capacity. Window, protected, and total sizes are conserved across promotion, demotion, update, delete, and eviction. `NewTinyLfuFunc` adds eviction vetoes with an LRU-compatible soft cap; vetoed candidates and victims are skipped during the eviction walk. Plain `All` walks window/probation/protected live lists in eviction order; the `_ts` twin snapshots under its read lock. Tests check those invariants and port Caffeine's reset/retracking, eviction-order, explicit-geometry, oversized-entry, oversized-promotion, and weighted-update cases. Paper tests use deterministic Zipf 0.9, scan, and phase-change workloads; Caffeine's bundled loop and scan traces provide replay coverage.

`tiny_lfu_ts` uses `sharded_hash_ts` value records with immediate visibility, striped bounded MPSC read rings, a reliable bounded write ring, and on-demand batched policy maintenance. Reads can drop policy events but never value lookups or exact stats; writes fall back to synchronous maintenance when full. Generation IDs coalesce superseded writes and prevent stale evictions/events from deleting or resurrecting newer values. Exact hits equal each read ring's monotonic accepted-offer count plus failed offers. Capacity is eventually enforced; vetoes retain the soft cap. `Frequency`/`Stats`/`All`/`Clear`/`Lock` drain maintenance. `NlPut`/`NlDelete`/`NlClear` are immediate under caller-held `Lock`; lookups may proceed during compounds. The standalone sketch remains RWMutex-guarded. Weighers run before value mutation; vetoes run during maintenance; neither may re-enter the cache. Tests cover ring reuse, overflow, stale events, exact stats, worker exit, weighted bounds, and concurrent Clear under `-race`.

The plain package remains synchronous and fixed-window. Neither version includes expiration, Caffeine's adaptive window climber, or randomized hash-flooding admission jitter.

Benchmark reality (Apple M4 Max, Go 1.27, OpenJDK 11.0.28, Caffeine 3.3.2-SNAPSHOT, 2026-11-19): matched 32,768-key JMH/Go harnesses measured Caffeine at 36.14 versus plain Go 18.25 Mops/s for single-thread hits, 32.13 versus 18.68 for updates, and 17.60 versus 15.02 for evicting puts. Eight-thread Caffeine reached 725.85/226.19/451.96 Mops/s for hits/updates/95%-read mixed, versus buffered Go's 14.22/8.76/16.66. Buffering improved the original Go concurrent results by 3.4x/1.9x/4.0x and eliminated hot-path allocations, but Caffeine remains faster due to lower map/atomic/scheduler costs. JOL and GC-stable HeapAlloc delta at 25,000 entries/capacity 50,000 measured 56 metadata bytes/entry for Caffeine, 48 for plain Go, and 145 for the buffered twin. Full methods, commands, and caveats are in both TinyLFU READMEs; matched sources are `tiny_lfu/bench_test.go` and `tiny_lfu/testdata/CaffeineBenchmark.java`.

Disk-cache replacement evaluation: synthetic 64-block traces showed hit-rate gains on B+tree-like Zipf (80.18% versus LRU 75.28%) and hash-like Zipf (61.10% versus 48.10%), with sequential/resident parity. A trial integration required preserving two LRU residency guarantees: a fresh block must survive its own insertion, and a hit must remain resident when maintenance runs above the soft cap. With those protected by temporary pins, both stores passed race-enabled crash/model suites. Real three-run means nevertheless rejected the switch: B+tree hot/cold/Zipf searches slowed 36.7%/37.3%/4.9%; hash hot/cold/Zipf slowed 29.0%/509.2%/412.6%. Immediate policy work and the extra protection lookup outweighed reduced reads. Both stores remain on LRU. TinyLFU metadata measured 48 bytes/entry versus LRU 64 at 25,000 entries/capacity 50,000, but the fixed sketch penalizes small caches. Evaluation sources and full tables are retained in `disk_eval_test.go`, store `BenchmarkSearchZipf` benchmarks, and both TinyLFU READMEs.
